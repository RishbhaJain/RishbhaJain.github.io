Project 0: Becoming Friends with Your Camera

This project explores how camera position and zoom affect perspective. All photos were shot on an iPhone 15.

Part 1: Selfie — The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

In the first picture, the face looks a little narrower and distorted. In the second picture, the proportions look more natural. This happens because when the subject is close, the camera is very sensitive to small changes in depth, such as the distance from the nose to the cheeks. A camera records angles, not “true size.”

apparent size ∝ actual size / distance from camera

When the camera is close, even a small depth difference changes this ratio a lot. Stepping back makes those depth differences less important, so the face looks more natural.

Part 2: Architectural Perspective Compression

In the first picture, taken from farther back while zoomed in, the building looks much flatter. In the second picture, taken from closer without zoom, the angles are much more pronounced. The reason is similar to the portrait example: changing the camera position changes the perspective, while zoom mainly changes the framing.

Part 3: The Dolly Zoom

We took these photos in a parking lot while walking backward and zooming in, keeping the subject’s face the same size and centered in the middle box of the iPhone camera grid. Even though the subject stays roughly the same size, the background appears to shift and recede.

Moving the camera backward changes the perspective of the scene, while zooming in only changes how much of that scene is included in the frame. As we move farther away, the distance between the subject and background becomes smaller compared with the total distance to the camera. Zooming keeps the subject the same size, but the background changes in size and position. Together, this creates the dolly zoom effect.
